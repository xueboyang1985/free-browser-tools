When to Use Each Format

JPG Compression

Best for photographs and complex images with many colors. A quality setting of 70-80% typically reduces file size by 60-80% with minimal visible difference.

PNG Compression

Best for screenshots, logos, and images with text or sharp edges. PNG compression is lossless — no quality loss, but smaller savings than JPG.

WebP Compression

Google's modern format. Typically 25-35% smaller than equivalent quality JPG. Supported in all modern browsers.

Pro tip: For web use, try WebP format first — it gives the best quality-to-size ratio. For printed materials or archiving, stick with high-quality JPG or PNG.
